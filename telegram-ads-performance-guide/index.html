How to Analyze Telegram Ads Performance

A practical framework for reading campaign data, finding where performance breaks down, and deciding what to test next — without confusing cheap views with valuable users.

By 007SaaS · Practical guide · Updated November 2026
In this guide

  • The metrics that matter
  • Diagnose the funnel
  • Decide what to change

1. Start with the outcome, not the dashboard

Before opening a report, write down the outcome the campaign is supposed to produce. A channel-growth campaign, a bot activation campaign and a revenue campaign should not be judged by the same primary KPI.

  • Channel growth: qualified new members, cost per join, member retention and downstream engagement.
  • Bot acquisition: bot starts, cost per start, completion of the first meaningful action and repeat usage.
  • Revenue: paid conversions, revenue, contribution margin and payback — not just views or joins.

Keep one primary KPI and a small set of diagnostic metrics. If the goal is paid customers, a low cost per subscriber is only an early signal, not proof of success.

2. Understand the core metrics

Telegram's official ad dashboard describes statistics including views and, for supported destinations, users who joined a channel or started a bot after viewing a sponsored message. The exact fields available can vary by ad format, account and interface. Check the current report in your own account before building a spreadsheet around a metric. Formulas worth keeping

CPM = Spend ÷ Impressions × 1,000
Cost per join = Spend ÷ Attributed new joins
CTR = Clicks ÷ Impressions × 100% (when clicks are available)
ROAS = Attributed revenue ÷ Ad spend
ROI = (Attributed gross profit − Ad spend) ÷ Ad spend

Do not use ROAS and ROI as synonyms. ROAS compares revenue with advertising cost; ROI should account for the relevant return and cost base. If you only know revenue and ad spend, report ROAS and state the calculation rather than implying profit.

3. Diagnose the funnel in order

A. Delivery is weak

Check whether the ad is active, approved, funded and actually receiving impressions. Review CPM, audience or placement scope, schedule and budget pacing. A low impression count is a delivery problem until proven otherwise — not automatically a copy problem.

B. Views are coming in, but few users take action

Review the promise in the ad text against the destination. Is the audience relevant? Does the channel or bot explain its value immediately? Is the first action obvious? Compare creative and audience tests separately so you can tell what caused the change.

C. Joins or starts are cheap, but quality is poor

Look beyond acquisition cost. Check day-1 and day-7 retention where available, channel post views, meaningful bot actions, lead quality or paid conversion. Cheap users who leave immediately may be more expensive in the long run.

D. Results look good, but revenue does not follow

Audit the full path from acquisition to monetization: onboarding, offer, pricing, sales response time, payment completion and attribution. Do not assign every later sale to the ad unless tracking supports that claim.

Important measurement note

Platform-attributed joins or bot starts are not the same as all downstream conversions. For revenue or external-site outcomes, use consistent campaign identifiers and your own analytics/CRM where possible. Document the attribution window and avoid adding platform counts to analytics counts.

4. Decide what to change — one variable at a time

Observed patternFirst hypothesis to testControlled next test
High CPM, low deliveryBid, eligible inventory or targeting may be limiting reach.Test a comparable audience/placement with a controlled CPM change.
Healthy views, weak joinsMessage, audience fit or destination promise is weak.Test a new message while holding targeting steady.
Good joins, weak retentionTraffic quality or onboarding mismatch.Test a narrower audience or improve the channel/bot first-run experience.
Good early results, rising costCreative fatigue, audience saturation or competition.Refresh the message or rotate a new audience; compare equivalent time windows.
Good acquisition, weak revenueMonetization or sales conversion bottleneck.Inspect activation, offer and sales follow-up before increasing spend.

Change one major variable per test where practical. If you change audience, message and bid at the same time, the result may improve — but you will not know why. Also note that Telegram's self-serve guidance says targeting parameters on an existing ad cannot be edited; use the platform's “Create a similar ad” flow when you need a different targeting setup. Check the current interface and rules for your account.

5. A repeatable weekly review workflow

  1. Export: collect spend, impressions/views, CPM and available result counts for the same date range.
  2. Normalize: align currency, timezone, campaign names, result definitions and attribution windows.
  3. Segment: compare by campaign, audience/placement, creative and date — avoid mixing unlike traffic sources.
  4. Calculate: CPM, cost per result, CTR where available, retention and ROAS/ROI only when the inputs support them.
  5. Diagnose: identify whether the main constraint is delivery, response, user quality or monetization.
  6. Act: pause clear losers, protect proven performers and launch a small controlled test.
  7. Record: write down the hypothesis, change, date and outcome so the next review builds on evidence.

6. Frequently asked questions

Is a lower CPM always better?

No. A lower CPM can buy cheaper exposure, but the audience may be less likely to join, activate or pay. Judge CPM alongside cost per qualified result and downstream quality.

Should I optimize for views or joins?

Use the metric closest to the business objective. Views help diagnose delivery; joins or bot starts are stronger intermediate outcomes; retention, qualified leads and revenue tell you whether the traffic matters.

Can I compare two campaigns with different date ranges?

Only with caution. Use comparable periods, normalize for spend and delivery, and account for different attribution windows, audience sizes and campaign maturity.

What if my dashboard does not show clicks or revenue?

Do not invent those metrics. Use the fields available in the platform and connect downstream analytics or CRM data where appropriate. Mark unavailable values as unavailable.

Where can I check Telegram's current ad rules?

Use the official Getting Started guide and Ad Policies and Guidelines. Platform features and eligible ad formats can change.

Sources and scope

This guide is an operating framework, not a statement that every Telegram ad account exposes identical metrics or features. Official platform references: Getting Started, Terms of Service, and Ad Policies. Always verify current settings and reporting in your own account.
